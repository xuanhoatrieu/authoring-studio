# Research and citations

Search current primary sources when facts, policies, product behaviour or dates may
have changed. Record publication date separately from access date and reference year.
A 2025 publication accessed in 2026 is not a 2026 publication. Preserve the legal
scope and effective period; verify current place names without rewriting historical
source titles. Prefer official policy, platform documentation and original studies.

Before citing, read the relevant passage. Store source identity in `references.yaml`
using CSL-compatible fields under `references`. Store research-only facts (retrieval
date, locator, conflicting evidence, verification status) in `evidence.yaml`.
Use a precise page, article, section, table or heading as the locator.
Do not store copied full copyrighted works in a distributable plugin.

Maintain stable source IDs; cite in Markdown with `[@source-id]`, optionally
`[@source-id, p. 12]`. A future processor can produce an author-date or numbered
style after that choice is agreed. Keep bibliography style provisional if undecided.

Evidence records distinguish `unverified`, `verified`, and `needs-update`.
Verification means the source supports the specific statement, not that the statement
appeared in a search result. Retain conflicting sources and resolve or qualify the
claim. Do not turn uncertain claims into publication-ready text.

Real examples require checked names, location and facts. Tourism awards or attractive
photos do not establish that a digital campaign improved commercial results.
When a source changes, use affected section IDs to identify what must be reviewed.
For third-party repository code or instructions, record license and attribution
before copying. The current plugin is independently authored; reference projects
are inspiration, not installed dependencies.
